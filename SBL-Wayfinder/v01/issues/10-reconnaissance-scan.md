# SBL | Research | What is the high-level read before deep research?

Type: research
Status: resolved
Resolved: 2025-10-01
Blocked by: —

## Question

Before committing to five deep primary-source investigations, what does a fast
reconnaissance scan say about SBL's feasibility, its prior art, and the shape of its
requirements? The purpose is to tell the project owner where the sharp edges are
early, so the deep research can be aimed rather than exhaustive.

## Answer

See `10-findings.md`. This is a **preliminary scan from model knowledge, not a
primary-source investigation** — every claim in it is a hypothesis for tickets
[SBL | Research | Is the core hypothesis already falsified?](01-is-the-core-hypothesis-already-falsified.md),
[SBL | Research | Does this already exist, and where is it used?](02-does-this-already-exist.md),
[SBL | Engineering | Can the first milestone be built with tools that exist?](03-can-the-first-milestone-be-built-with-tools-that-exist.md),
[SBL | Engineering | What does it take to reach the final state?](04-what-does-it-take-to-reach-the-final-state.md)
and [SBL | Research | Would anyone adopt a shared semantic ID space?](05-would-anyone-adopt-a-shared-semantic-id-space.md)
to confirm, sharpen, or overturn.

The three load-bearing judgements:

1. **Engineering feasibility: high.** Every pipeline component exists.
2. **Scientific novelty: low to moderate, and the one genuinely testable claim is
   untested.** The representation is a recombination of existing parts.
3. **Adoption feasibility: very low.** The interoperability claim is unfalsifiable at
   the design doc's own target scale.

The recommended reframe is in the artifact's final section: drop the universal-layer
framing, pick one falsifiable claim, and test that.
